Mount Pleasant Bachelorette Weekend

Updated Oct 28, 2026|7 min read

A good Mount Pleasant bachelorette weekend runs on water. Paddle the creek in the morning, spend the afternoon on a deck or a beach, and save downtown for the one night that needs it. Plan it in that order and the town does the rest.

Mount Pleasant sits across the harbor from Charleston, fifteen minutes over the Ravenel Bridge. Shem Creek is the center of it, a working creek where shrimp boats tie up beside restaurants stacked two and three decks high. This guide covers what to do, where to eat, where to stay, a two-day plan, and the local rules worth knowing before anyone books a house. For the downtown side of the trip, start with our Charleston bachelorette guide.

Why Mount Pleasant works (and where it falls short)

The town gives a group three things downtown can't: dolphins within paddling distance, sunset decks with no dress code, and hotels with harbor views at lower prices than the peninsula. Spring and fall are the best months. Summer works if the paddling happens early and the group spends midday on the beach or by the pool.

The shortfall is nightlife. Mount Pleasant goes quiet earlier than King Street, and nothing here comes close to a downtown dance floor. Keep the big night on the peninsula, rideshare over the bridge, and use Mount Pleasant for everything else. Groups that try to do both sides of the bridge on the same evening spend the night in the car.

The best water for a bachelorette group

There are four places to get on or beside the water, and each one suits a different part of the day.

Shem Creek. Calm, protected water lined with shrimp boats, plus dolphins near the mouth of the creek most mornings. It's the easiest place in the area to put a nervous first-timer on a paddleboard.

Sullivan's Island. Mount Pleasant has no ocean beach of its own. Sullivan's is ten minutes over the causeway and quieter than Folly, with wide sand and a view of ships entering the harbor. Burgers and drinks at Poe's Tavern on Middle Street make the afternoon.

Charleston Harbor. Cruises leave from Patriots Point next to the USS Yorktown, so the group sees the skyline and the Battery from the water without driving downtown. Book a private charter out of the Charleston Harbor Marina if the group wants the boat to itself.

Pitt Street Bridge. The old trolley bridge to Sullivan's Island is now a palm-lined park at the end of the Old Village, with Fort Sumter on the horizon. It closed for repairs in recent years, so check that it's open before you plan a sunset there.

Daytime Mount Pleasant bachelorette ideas

Give each day one booked activity and let the rest happen around it. These are the anchors that work best for a group.

Kayak with Coastal Expeditions. Its outpost on Mill Street sits up the creek, away from the restaurant traffic, and has been running Shem Creek tours since 1992. Rent kayaks or paddleboards for a self-guided loop, or book the guided tour out to the Crab Bank bird sanctuary if nobody has paddled before. Walk-ups are welcome, but text ahead to have boats ready for a big group.

Boone Hall Plantation. The drive in runs under an avenue of live oaks planted in the 1740s. Allow time for the house tour and the Gullah culture presentation. The property also hosts seasonal festivals that are worth checking against your dates.

Memorial Waterfront Park. The Mount Pleasant Pier runs 1,250 feet into the harbor directly under the Ravenel Bridge. It's the group photo of the weekend. Grab soft-serve from the River Watch Cafe at the foot of the pier, and come back after dinner if you like, since the pier stays open until 10 p.m.

The Old Village. Order a milkshake at the Pitt Street Pharmacy soda fountain, which has been pouring since 1937. Then walk the shaded streets down to the water. It's the slowest, prettiest hour of the trip.

Shem Creek Park boardwalk. A free walk out through the marsh with the restaurant decks behind you and pelicans working the creek. Go in the last hour of light.

The private show comes to you

Mount Pleasant has no club for this, so Michael At Your Place brings the show to your rental or hotel suite. One entertainer performs a traditional, interactive show, booked for brunch or late night.

It's $200 for up to 90 minutes, paid on arrival by cash, Venmo, or Cash App. Last-minute dates are welcome, so call or text 843-360-9438.

Where to eat, from brunch to the last deck

Seafood on the creek and Southern brunch on Coleman Boulevard. Book ahead for groups larger than six, especially on weekends.

Brunch

Page's Okra Grill at 302 Coleman Blvd is the town's brunch institution and a regular winner of the local best-brunch vote. Expect shrimp and grits, chicken and waffles, and a Sunday wait, so get there early for a long table. For a slower start, Coleman Public House at 427 W Coleman Blvd pairs a weekend brunch with a deep beer list. Vicious Biscuit stacks its scratch biscuits high enough to need a knife, with a cocktail menu to match.

Lunch and sunset on the creek

The creek restaurants sit side by side, so the group can walk from one to the next. Red's Ice House has two levels of deck, fried shrimp, and the most relaxed dress code on the water. Shem Creek Bar & Grill adds an oyster bar and plenty of outdoor seating. As the light drops, move to the two-story deck at Saltwater Cowboys or the cocktail list at Tavern & Table, which also runs one of the most-booked brunches in town.

The celebration dinner

Water's Edge is the dressier option on the creek, with a waterfront bar and tables outside. R.B.'s Seafood Restaurant has served fried seafood on the creek since 1979 and still has one of the best sunset views. For a quieter, more refined meal off the water, book Langdon's and its seasonal Southern menu.

Where to stay across the harbor

Mount Pleasant has more waterfront hotels than Folly but fewer whole-house rentals, so decide early which one your group needs.

The Shem Creek Inn on Shrimp Boat Lane puts every restaurant on the creek within walking distance. The Beach Club at Charleston Harbor Resort and Marina on Patriots Point Road has a harbor-front pool and a small private beach facing the skyline, and its sister hotel Harborside sits on the same property at a lower price. A block of rooms keeps the group together without any of the rental questions.

A house near the Old Village or Coleman Boulevard gives everyone one kitchen, one porch, and room for a private show. Mount Pleasant caps short-term rental permits, though, and many neighborhood HOAs ban short-term rentals outright. Ask the host for the town permit number and tell them it's a bachelorette group before you pay a deposit.

Rules to know before you book

On the beach. Sullivan's Island and Isle of Palms both ban alcohol and glass on the sand, and both enforce it with patrols and steep fines. Sullivan's also bans balloons and foam, so pack fabric banners instead. Its beach closes at 11 p.m.

Rentals. A listing without a Mount Pleasant permit can be shut down mid-stay. Read the house rules on noise and guest counts, and confirm parking for every car.

Getting around. Shem Creek lots fill on weekend evenings, so carpool or rideshare in. The Ravenel Bridge is the only quick route downtown. Plan a rideshare for any night that includes drinks.

Rules come from the Town of Mount Pleasant, the Town of Sullivan's Island, and the City of Isle of Palms, and they change. Check each town's website before the trip. All picks here are our own, and no business paid to be listed.
