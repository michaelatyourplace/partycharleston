Where To Stay for a Charleston Bachelorette Party

Updated 2026|8 min read

Most groups picture a house downtown, and that is still the prime pick. But plenty of the groups we meet go out on King Street and sleep somewhere else: Park Circle, a hotel off Dorchester Road, Mount Pleasant, or a beach house on Isle of Palms or Folly Beach. Where you stay decides how much of the weekend is spent walking, riding, or sitting on the sand, so pick the area before you pick the house.

Once the address is set, the rest of the plan is in our Charleston bachelorette party ideas guide.

Downtown Is the Prime Pick

Stay on the peninsula and the weekend runs on foot. Brunch, King Street shopping, the Market, the rooftops, and the bars are all a walk or a five-minute ride apart, nobody needs a designated driver, and the group gets home together at the end of the night. It costs more than anywhere else on this page, and it books first.

Best For Most Groups

A Rental House or Airbnb

One house beats a row of hotel rooms for a bachelorette. Everyone gets ready together, there is a kitchen for the morning after, and there is room for a decor setup at the house. The East Bay rowhouse rentals are multi-bedroom historic homes that sleep 8 to 12, at roughly $700 to $1,200 or more a night for the whole house, which splits well across a group.

Whole-house listings downtown are scarcer than in most cities because Charleston limits short-term rentals. Search Airbnb and Vrbo early, and ask the host for the city permit number before you send a deposit.

French Quarter

A Boutique Hotel With a Rooftop

Hotels work better when the group is small, when people are arriving on different days, or when some of the crew want their own bathroom. The Vendue is an art-filled boutique hotel two blocks from the water, and its rooftop bar makes an easy meeting point before dinner. Rooms run about $250 to $450 or more a night.

East Bay Street

A Historic Inn on the Harbor

HarbourView Inn puts the group on the waterfront, with the Market, Rainbow Row, and King Street all under ten minutes away on foot. Rooms run about $250 to $500 or more a night. Book a block of rooms on the same floor so the group is not spread across the building.

Staying Off the Peninsula

These areas trade the walk home for more space and a lower nightly rate. The catch is the ride: plan on a car each way for every outing, and read the bachelorette transportation guide before you commit to a house that is 20 minutes out.

About 15 minutes to King Street

Park Circle

North Charleston's Park Circle is the pick for groups who want a neighborhood of their own. It sits under ten miles north of downtown, and East Montague Avenue has its own strip of restaurants, breweries, and bars, with spots like EVO Pizzeria and Madra Rua, so a low-key first night never has to leave the neighborhood. Rental houses here are larger and cheaper than their downtown equivalents. The trade-off is the ride back from King Street after last call.

About 20 minutes to King Street

Dorchester Road & the Airport Area

The hotels along Dorchester Road and around the airport are the budget play. Rooms cost a fraction of a downtown hotel, parking is free, and the group is minutes from baggage claim on arrival and departure days. Nothing nearby is walkable for a night out, so this works best for groups who plan to be downtown or on a boat all day and only need clean beds, or for the friends flying in late Friday.

10 to 20 minutes to King Street

Mount Pleasant

Across the Ravenel Bridge, Mount Pleasant splits the difference between downtown and the beach. Shem Creek has waterfront bars and the kayak tours, the harbor resorts at Patriots Point are a water taxi ride from Waterfront Park, and Isle of Palms and Sullivan's Island are a short drive the other way. Rentals are limited by the town, so most groups here book hotel rooms. Our Mount Pleasant bachelorette guide covers the area in full.

10 to 15 minutes to King Street

West Ashley & James Island

West Ashley is the closest off-peninsula option and the easiest ride home at night. James Island sits between downtown and Folly Beach, which makes it a smart base for a group that wants one night on King Street and one day on the sand without paying beachfront rates.

Beach Houses for a Beach-First Weekend

If the bride wants sand, a pool, and a porch more than a bar crawl, stay at the beach and make downtown the one big night. Both islands are roughly 25 to 30 minutes from King Street without traffic, and summer weekend traffic can add to that.

25 to 30 minutes to King Street

Isle of Palms

Isle of Palms is the polished option, with big rental homes built for groups, wide beaches, and a small strip of bars and restaurants at Front Beach. Wild Dunes Resort at the north end has hotel rooms, condos, and vacation homes with pools and a spa, and the Seaside Inn sits right on the oceanfront. It is the quieter of the two islands at night.

25 to 30 minutes to King Street

Folly Beach

Folly is the laid-back, barefoot island. Center Street packs bars, live music, and late-night food into a few walkable blocks, so a Folly group can have a full night out without crossing a bridge. Beach houses line both ends of the island, and Tides Folly Beach is the oceanfront hotel at the foot of Center Street, next to the pier.

Sullivan's Island Is for Day Trips

Sullivan's Island has some of the best restaurants and prettiest beaches in the area, but the town does not allow vacation rentals. Visit for lunch and a beach afternoon, and sleep somewhere else.

Booking Tips That Protect the Weekend

Book three months out or more. Downtown weekends fill fast, and fall is wedding season, so the good group houses go early. Spring and fall are the peak, and the same house often costs less on a summer or winter weekend.

Count beds, not the "sleeps" number. A house that sleeps twelve may be counting pull-out sofas and bunk rooms. Ask for the bed list and match it to who is willing to share.

Read the house rules before you pay. Many Charleston rentals post quiet hours and ban events, extra guests, and outside vendors. If the plan includes a decorator, a private chef, or entertainment at the house, clear it with the host in writing first.

Check the permit and the parking. Rental rules differ in every town here, and a listing without a local permit can be cancelled on you. Ask how many cars fit at the house too, since street parking downtown and at the beaches is limited.
