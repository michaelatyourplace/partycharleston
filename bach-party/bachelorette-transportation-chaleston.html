Charleston Bachelorette Transportation

Updated 2026|6 min read

A bachelorette weekend in Charleston comes down to five trips: the airport run, daytime hops around the peninsula, a boat or beach day, the big night out, and the ride home. This page covers what to book for each one, what it costs, and how many cars your group needs.

The Rides Most Groups Book

Downtown parking is tight and pricey. Skip the rental car and let someone else drive.

Rideshare

Lyft

The easiest way to hop between the house, restaurants, and bars downtown without worrying about parking or a designated driver. A standard car seats four; request an XL for six.

Estimated cost: $8–$20 per ride for short trips around downtown

Rideshare

Uber

Widely available across the peninsula and Mount Pleasant. Keep both apps on a couple of phones and book whichever shows the shorter wait.

Estimated cost: $8–$20 per ride for short trips around downtown

Group Transport

Party Bus

For bigger crews who want the party to keep going between stops. A party bus or limo keeps everyone together for the night, with no splitting into separate cars.

Estimated cost: $200–$400 per hour

The Weekend, One Ride at a Time

Five trips, in the order you will take them.

  1. Arrival Day

    Airport to the House

    Charleston International (CHS) sits about 12 miles from downtown, a 20 to 30 minute ride that runs roughly $25 to $40 in a standard car. Uber and Lyft both pick up at the airport's marked rideshare zone, and the app walks you to it.

    If flights land within an hour of each other, have the early arrivals wait at baggage claim and split XLs instead of paying for five separate cars. For a bigger entrance, Charleston Black Cab Co. handles airport transportation along with its limo vans and party buses, so the weekend can start at the curb.

  2. Daytime

    Short Hops on the Peninsula

    Most of the daytime plan is walkable. Brunch, King Street shopping, the Market, and the rooftops all sit within a mile or so of each other, so save the rides for heels, heat, and rain.

    The DASH shuttle is free and runs three loops around the peninsula, including one along Meeting and King. Pedicabs are the fun option: Charleston Rickshaw Company, Charleston Pedicab, and Charleston Bike Taxi all work downtown, charge about $8 per person for each ten minutes, and seat two or three per bike. A bachelorette group in a convoy of them makes a good photo.

  3. Boat Day or Beach Day

    Marinas, the Harbor & the Beaches

    Boats do not wait. Check the departure dock on your confirmation, since charters and party boats leave from different marinas on the Ashley River side of downtown and from Mount Pleasant, and set the drop-off for 20 minutes before departure.

    For a cheap harbor crossing, the Charleston Water Taxi loops hourly between Waterfront Park, the Aquarium, Patriots Point, and Charleston Harbor Resort on an all-day pass that costs about $14. It runs in daylight hours only, so it will not get you home after dinner.

    Folly Beach and Isle of Palms are each about 25 to 30 minutes from downtown. Getting a ride out is easy. Getting one back on a busy afternoon takes longer and costs more, so schedule the return ahead in the app or book a van for the day. Groups staying across the bridge should read the Mount Pleasant bachelorette guide.

  4. The Big Night

    One Vehicle for the Whole Crew

    This is the night to book a driver. A party bus or limo runs $200 to $400 an hour in Charleston, usually with a two-hour minimum, and many are BYOB with coolers and ice already on board. Ask about the drink policy, the gratuity, and where the bus can legally stop near your restaurant before you pay a deposit.

    Charleston Black Cab Co. runs everything from limo vans to coaches that hold up to 38. Double Black Transportation, Charleston Downtown Limo, and Graffiti Limo, whose bus is painted by a local artist, also book bachelorette groups.

    The smart play is to use the bus for the house, dinner, and the first bar, then release it and walk the rest of the King Street bar crawl. If the entertainment is coming to you, such as the bachelorette brunch show, that is one stop with no ride to plan at all.

  5. Last Call

    The Ride Home

    Rideshare prices jump when the bars let out at 2 a.m. Leave 20 minutes before close, or grab late-night food and request the car after the rush.

    Walk a block off King Street before you request so the driver can find you, put one person in each car in charge of the address, and share the trip status in the group chat. Pedicabs also work the King Street crowd at night and suit a short ride back to a downtown rental.
